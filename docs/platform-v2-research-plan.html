1. How to read this

Every research item below has the same four parts, because a research task that does not name the decision it unblocks is just reading:

PartMeaning
QuestionThe specific thing we do not currently know.
MethodHow we find out — a query against real data, a spike, a vendor comparison, a labelling session with the two reps.
DeliverableThe artifact that ends the item. Usually a short written finding or a number.
UnblocksThe build decision that cannot responsibly be made until the deliverable exists.

Items carry a size estimate: S under a day, M one to three days, L more than three days. These are research sizes, not implementation sizes.

3. Three things I am challenging

3.1 "Only good news" is inverted for the cargo business proposal

The vision asks for a ++ … -- score per article, on the reasoning that bad news cannot be a sales signal because a company cutting costs will not buy. Knowing what ProAir sells turns that premise around.

Air charter is bought under duress. Urgent and oversized freight moves by air when something has gone wrong: a line is about to stop, a supplier's plant burned, a port or rail network is struck, a vessel is delayed, a supplier filed for insolvency, a recall needs parts distributed now. ProAir's stated edge — operational excellence and global procedural knowledge rather than price — is the profile of a business selling into urgency. Price-insensitivity is a symptom of crisis-driven demand: when a line is down at tens of thousands of euros an hour, nobody negotiates freight rates.

But it is almost never the buyer's own bad news. This is the distinction that rescues the original intuition. A fire at a tier-one supplier is not a signal about that supplier — it is a signal about every OEM downstream of it. A dock strike is not a signal about the port. So the rule is not "good news only", it is:

The useful axis is what the event does to spend in our category. Does it create demand (incident, strike, insolvency upstream, recall, project milestone, roadshow, tournament), freeze it (the buyer's own insolvency or site closure), or not affect it? Same Mistral call, no extra pass, no sentiment axis. Negative-and-freezing events suppress outreach; negative-and-creating events are the most valuable signals ProAir can receive. A single ++ … -- score cannot express that difference, which is the concrete reason not to build it.

Announced strikes deserve a specific mention as probably the best-fit signal class available. Verdi and EVG announce industrial action days in advance. The announcements are dated, precisely scoped to sites or networks, thoroughly covered in German press, and they create immediate, predictable air-freight demand. Forward-looking and unambiguous — rare properties in a news signal.

The passenger side is more mixed, and there the original intuition holds better: M&A and roadshows, IPOs, tournament qualification, tour announcements and large corporate events are genuinely good news that creates group and VIP demand. The exceptions are airline insolvencies, route cancellations and carrier strikes — bad news for the airline, direct demand for a charter operator — and crisis evacuation or repatriation, where a German operator with its own AOC has a specific advantage.

The bigger consequence. Because the news subject and the buyer are usually different entities, and the news subject is often not a company at all (a port, an airport, a rail operator, a canal, a region), the platform's company-centric model does not fit the cargo business. That is not a §3 challenge — it is large enough to be its own track. See Track H.

3.2 The RSS firehose page and "News-Websites" are one feature decided

The vision lists them separately. A standalone page showing all latest German news unfiltered inverts the product's own value — filtering is what NewsAtlas is for, and an unfiltered feed is the thing the reps already have. As agreed, they merge into one section whose job is source discovery: browse a portal, see what it actually publishes, then attach it to the workspace default, a company, or a topic. It also absorbs the existing Settings → Sources tab and the source-precision panel, so there is one place to manage sources rather than three.

3.3 The apollo.io rebuild is out decided

Dropped for v2. Recording the reasoning so it does not resurface by accident: Apollo's moat is data licensing and refresh, not software; German B2B cold email under §7 UWG has no blanket legitimate-interest exemption, so an auto-send button would hand ProAir legal exposure; and sending cold volume would burn the same domain the daily digest goes out on. The prepare-and-copy workflow gets most of the value with none of that.

4. Phase 0 — Measurement foundation Blocks everything

Nothing else in this document can be decided honestly without numbers, and we currently have no way to get them. This is the one phase with a hard ordering constraint.

Confirmed gap: NewsAtlas has no data export. CSV support is import-only (backend/app/api/target_companies.py:162). The only read surface for raw pipeline data is GET /articles/skipped — admin-only and capped at 200 rows (backend/app/api/articles.py:36). There is no way to answer "what does our score distribution look like" from the running system.

R0.1 Analytical export M

Question: What is the smallest export that answers every measurement question in this document without becoming a reporting feature we then have to maintain?

Method: Enumerate the fields each item below needs — for articles: fetched_at, published_at, source, source_name, skip_reason, triage_reason, content_enriched; for signals: relevance_score, signal_type, confidence, status, token counts, emailed_at. Design one admin CSV/JSON export per entity with a date range. Check what the existing ai_usage_logs and news_source_usage_logs tables already cover so we do not duplicate them.

Deliverable: A short spec for the export endpoints plus a decision on whether this is a permanent admin feature or a throwaway script run against the production database.

Unblocks: Every item marked needs measurement.

My recommendation: build it as a real, permanent admin export. We will want to re-measure after every prompt change in Track A, and a throwaway script that needs database access each time will not get run.

R0.2 Baseline funnel and score distribution M needs measurement

Question: What does the pipeline actually do today? Specifically: the histogram of relevance_score across all signals; the same split by signal_type, confidence, source and is_headline_only; articles fetched per company per month; the drop rates at each funnel stage (URL dedupe → title collapse → cap → embedding dedupe → triage → summarize); and the dismissal rate per score band.

Method: Run the R0.1 export over the full history and produce one analysis notebook.

Deliverable: A baseline report with the histograms. This is the "before" picture that every Track A change is measured against.

Unblocks: A1, A2, A3, B1, C1. Also tells us whether the 3/5–4/5 clustering you observe is real and how severe it is.

R0.3 Cost model against the €20 ceiling S

Question: What does one ingestion run cost today, broken down by stage (embedding, triage, summarization), and how much headroom does €20/month leave for richer prompts?

Method: Confirm current Mistral list prices per model from the vendor (do not assume — they change). Cross them with the real token counts we already store per signal (Signal.prompt_tokens / completion_tokens) and the aggregate ai_usage_logs. Model the marginal cost of the Track A prompt changes before writing them.

Deliverable: A cost-per-run and cost-per-month model with a sensitivity table: what happens at 2× companies, 2× output tokens, or a model swap.

Unblocks: A2, A4, A5, C1. Concretely: it tells us whether a two-axis prompt fits in budget before we design one.

5. Track A — Making the score discriminate

This is the highest-value track. Everything downstream — the digest, the timeline, the Salesforce push — ranks on relevance_score, so a score that clusters at 3–4 makes all of them weaker. Three concrete causes are already visible in the code.

A1 How many 3s are not judgements? S needs measurement

Question: What share of signals scored 3 were actually scored by the model, versus silently defaulted?

Confirmed defect: SummaryResult.relevance_score: int = 3 (backend/app/services/ai_client.py:218). If Mistral omits the key or returns something unparseable, the signal silently becomes a 3. The same pattern applies to confidence = "medium" and signal_type = "other". Unlike the signal_type and confidence validators, which at least log a warning on an unrecognized value, a missing field logs nothing at all. We cannot currently distinguish "the model judged this a 3" from "the model said nothing."

Method: Add temporary instrumentation that records whether each field was present in the raw model response, run it for one ingestion cycle, and measure. Also replay a sample of stored articles to see how often the model returns malformed JSON.

Deliverable: The contamination rate. Plus a decision on the right fix — make the field required and treat its absence as a retry or an error, rather than defaulting into the middle of the scale.

Unblocks: A2. If contamination turns out to be a meaningful share of the 3s, part of the clustering problem is a bug and not a prompt problem, and the prompt work gets cheaper.

A2 A rubric that discriminates M

Question: What scoring design actually spreads signals across the range?

Two design defects in the current prompt (ai_client.py:52, and the parallel theme prompt at :147): (a) only the endpoints are anchored — 5 is "a clear, immediate buying trigger… today", 1 is "tangentially related background news", and 2, 3 and 4 are left undefined. A model given a 1–5 scale with unanchored middle values regresses toward the middle-high band, which is exactly the clustering you see. (b) One number carries two independent questions: is this a strong event? and does it fit our offering? A major event unrelated to ProAir and a minor event perfectly matched both land at 3–4.

Method: Research two candidate designs and test both against the A3 gold set. Candidate 1: keep one score but write explicit behavioural anchors for all five levels. Candidate 2: split into two axes — event strength and offering fit — and derive the displayed score from them. Measure output-token cost of each against the R0.3 model. Also review whether forced-choice or comparative prompting reduces central tendency more cheaply than anchoring.

Deliverable: A recommended rubric with measured score spread and cost delta versus today.

Unblocks: B1 (whether a 4/5 floor is meaningful), and the entire digest rule.

Related finding: theme_match_min_relevance_score defaults to 3 (models/workspace_settings.py), which places the topic quality floor exactly at the mode of the distribution — so it filters almost nothing. Company signals have no floor at all. Both need revisiting once the rubric changes.

A3 A gold set, or we are guessing M

Question: What do the two reps consider a genuinely actionable signal? Without this, "the score is wrong" has no referent and every prompt change is an opinion.

Method: Export roughly 100–150 real signals spanning the current score range. Both reps independently label each as act today / worth knowing / noise, and note the reason. Measure inter-rater agreement — where they disagree is itself a finding about what the product should optimize for. Build a small offline harness that replays a prompt against the gold set and reports agreement with the human labels.

Deliverable: A labelled evaluation set committed to the repo, plus a replay harness that runs on demand. Every later prompt change reports its score against it.

Unblocks: A2, A4, A5, and any future prompt work. This is the single most reusable artifact in the plan.

Cost note: replaying a 150-item gold set is a real Mistral spend against a €20 ceiling. R0.3 must size this, and the harness should cache responses so a re-run of an unchanged prompt costs nothing.

A4 Rebuilding the event taxonomy L

Question: What are the event classes that actually predict a charter booking, and can a model classify them reliably plus assign a budget direction?

The current taxonomy is for a different business. _SIGNAL_TYPES (ai_client.py:15) is funding, leadership_change, expansion, hiring_surge, layoffs, product_launch, partnership, competitor_mention, other. That is a B2B software seller's list. Essentially none of it names a charter trigger. This is a plausible contributing cause of the 3/5–4/5 clustering all by itself: the model is being asked to score charter opportunities against a rubric written for software sales, so it has no vocabulary for what makes one article better than another and falls back on generic business importance.

Method: Derive the taxonomy from ProAir's actual won business rather than from imagination — review the last 12–24 months of bookings and ask, for each, what event caused it and whether that event was reported in the press. That backward pass is the only way to know which classes matter and in what proportion. Candidate classes to test: production disruption / line-down, supplier incident (fire, explosion, flood), announced strike, insolvency (upstream and at the buyer, which are opposite signals), recall, oversized project milestone (plant construction, turbine or transformer delivery, mining and energy projects), customs or port congestion, airline route cancellation or carrier insolvency, M&A and roadshow, sports qualification and fixtures, tour announcement, evacuation and humanitarian response. Measure how much still lands in other — that is the tell for a taxonomy that is still too narrow. Then test whether budget direction (create / freeze / no effect) is reliable enough to act on, validated against the A3 labels.

Deliverable: A taxonomy grounded in won deals, with measured per-class classification reliability, and a recommendation on whether budget direction is trustworthy or should stay advisory.

Unblocks: Track C, Track H, and the A2 rubric — a scoring prompt cannot discriminate well while its own event vocabulary is wrong. Replaces the sentiment feature outright.

Design note: the mapping from event class to opportunity-or-suppressor is ProAir-specific and belongs in workspace settings, not hardcoded in a prompt. Cargo and passenger will want different mappings, which is an argument for making the class-to-action mapping configurable per business line rather than global.

A5 Cost-quality parity per pipeline stage M

Question: Where is the best trade between tokens spent and quality gained — the explicit goal you set for the €20 budget?

Method: The pipeline already runs three different models (mistral_model, mistral_triage_model, mistral_embed_model). Using the A3 harness, sweep each stage: does a larger triage model reduce false negatives enough to pay for itself? Does a smaller summarization model lose measurable quality? What does the ai_feedback_note steering mechanism (services/feedback.py) contribute, and does prompt length there earn its tokens? Evaluate prompt caching or shorter system prompts as a pure cost win. Quantify what enabling snippet enrichment would cost given teaser-only sources are now accepted.

Deliverable: A per-stage recommendation with measured quality-per-euro, and a configuration that fits €20/month with headroom.

Unblocks: The budget envelope for every other AI-touching feature.

6. Track B — The daily digest

Rule as decided: top 10, never below 4/5. Today's digest is different — it mails every un-emailed signal, ordered by score, filtered per user by their follows (services/digest.py). There is no quality floor at all.

B1 Does the rule produce an empty email? S needs measurement

Question: How many signals per day would clear a 4/5 floor today, and how would that change under the A2 rubric?

Method: Replay the rule against the R0.2 history. Produce a per-day count over the last 90 days for both the current scoring and, once A2 lands, the revised scoring.

Deliverable: A distribution of daily digest sizes, including how many days would send zero.

Unblocks: Whether the floor is 4, or adaptive.

The risk this exists to catch: if today's scores cluster at 3–4, a hard 4/5 floor could plausibly yield a nearly empty digest most days — and a daily email that is usually empty gets ignored, then filtered. The rule is right in principle; whether the threshold is right is an empirical question, and A2 may move the whole distribution underneath it. B1 must be re-run after A2.

B2 Digest design research M

Question: What does a rep need to see to act directly from the email, and what happens on a quiet day?

Method: Review the current digest rendering against the two reps' actual workflow. Decide the empty-day behaviour, the carry-over rule (a 5/5 signal that appeared yesterday and was not acted on — repeat or drop?), and whether the new digest replaces the existing one or runs beside it. Check how the per-user CompanyFollow / ThemeFollow filtering interacts with a "top 10" cap — top 10 globally, or top 10 per recipient?

Deliverable: A digest spec including empty-state copy in both languages.

Unblocks: Implementation.

My recommendation on the empty day: send the email anyway with an explicit "nothing above the bar today" line. Silence is indistinguishable from a broken pipeline, and with two users, a week of silence is a week nobody notices ingestion has stopped.
A daily digest is the wrong channel for half the business. Group, VIP and project-cargo opportunities have weeks of lead time and suit a 07:00 email perfectly. Disruption signals do not: a line-down or AOG decision is made within hours, and the shipper's logistics team knows before the press does. That does not make news a useless sensor — a plant fire is reported within hours, recovery shipments run for weeks, and being the broker who calls first with capacity is exactly the operational edge ProAir claims — but it does mean the disruption class needs a faster path than the morning digest. See H3.

B3 Transactional email provider evaluation S

Question: Should the digest keep going out over raw SMTP?

Current state: services/email_client.py is a thin smtplib wrapper. No bounce handling, no delivery tracking, no retry, no DKIM/SPF story in the repo. A failed send raises EmailClientError and the digest for that recipient is simply lost. For a daily email that is the product's main output surface, that is fragile.

Method: Compare Postmark, SES and Brevo on free-tier limits at this volume (2 recipients, daily), German or EU data residency, bounce and delivery webhooks, and integration effort against the existing EmailClient seam — which was deliberately built so the provider is a credential swap.

Deliverable: A recommendation with a cost line. At two recipients this may well land inside a free tier.

Unblocks: Whether B2 also carries a provider migration.

You noted this is "likely not necessary" given the copy-paste outreach workflow. Agreed that it is not needed for outreach — but the digest itself is still a daily email whose failure is currently silent, so I would keep this item scoped small and do it anyway.

7. Track C — Company event timeline

The "gute Zeiten / schlechte Zeiten" idea, rebuilt on events rather than sentiment per §3.1.

C1 Is there enough data for a timeline? S needs measurement

Question: How many signals does a typical tracked company accumulate per quarter?

Method: From R0.2, compute the per-company signal count distribution. Note that max_articles_per_company_per_run defaults to 10 and triage discards a further share, so the ceiling may be low.

Deliverable: The distribution, with an explicit finding on what share of companies have enough events for a timeline to say anything.

Unblocks: Whether Track C ships at all. If the median company produces two signals a quarter, a timeline is a chart of nothing and the honest answer is to cut it.

C2 What does the timeline show? S

Question: What representation actually helps a rep decide "approach now" versus "wait"?

Method: Sketch two or three representations against real company histories from the export — an event strip with typed markers, a rolling budget-direction indicator, a plain reverse-chronological list with type badges — and review with the reps. Decide whether the timeline is its own page or a panel on the existing company detail view.

Deliverable: A chosen representation with a real-data mockup.

Unblocks: Implementation.

Worth stating plainly: the plain list may win. With a handful of events per company per quarter, a chart adds ceremony without adding information, and a list with clear type badges and dates is both cheaper and more honest about the data density.

8. Track D — Salesforce end-to-end

Scope as decided: one-way push. If the company is absent in Salesforce, create it; if present, create a Task. The company list keeps arriving by CSV.

D1 Auth, objects and limits S

Question: What exactly does the working Python script do, and what are the API constraints around it?

Method: Document the existing connected-app / JWT bearer flow as the reference implementation. Confirm the Sales Cloud API request limits at this volume, the required object permissions for the API user, and whether a sandbox exists to develop against. Decide credential storage — the repo already has an encrypted-settings pattern for the Mistral key that this should reuse rather than reinvent.

Deliverable: An integration brief covering auth, permissions, limits and the sandbox story.

Unblocks: D2, D3.

D2 Company matching, now mostly solved S

Question: How do we know that a company in NewsAtlas is a given Salesforce Account, reliably enough to write records automatically?

Resolved by the CSV. The Salesforce export can carry the Account ID. Storing it on TargetCompany at import time means matching happens exactly once, under human supervision, on data that came out of Salesforce itself — instead of repeatedly at push time by fuzzy name comparison. This removes the failure mode that would otherwise have defined the track: a wrong match writing a Task onto the wrong customer, or a missed match creating duplicate Accounts in the CRM of record.

Method: Confirm the exact export column and add it to the import column-mapping UI, which already guesses header names (services/target_company_import.py). Decide the behaviour for the remaining cases: a company added manually in NewsAtlas with no Salesforce counterpart, an Account deleted or merged in Salesforce after import (the ID then resolves to nothing, or redirects to the surviving record), and a re-import that changes the ID. Verify that a stored ID still resolves before writing, and define what the UI shows when it does not.

Deliverable: An import mapping spec plus the defined behaviour for unmatched, stale and merged Accounts.

Unblocks: D3.

Recommendation: a company without a Salesforce ID should not be pushable at all. An explicit "not linked to Salesforce — re-import to link" state is far better than falling back to name matching, which would quietly reintroduce every risk the ID removes.

D3 Field mapping and idempotency M

Question: What exactly gets written, and what happens when the same signal is pushed twice?

Method: Define the field mapping for both paths (create Account, create Task on existing Account) including which required Salesforce fields have no NewsAtlas equivalent. Design idempotency — an external key on the Task, or a local record of what has been pushed — so a double click or a retry does not create two Tasks. Define ownership and assignment: which Salesforce user owns a created record. Decide the failure model: partial failures, validation-rule rejections, and how they surface in the UI.

Deliverable: A field mapping table and a written failure and retry model.

Unblocks: Implementation.

D4 Copy-paste that keeps its formatting S

Question: How do we put a formatted outreach snippet on the clipboard so it pastes cleanly into Outlook and Salesforce?

Method: Research the async Clipboard API writing both text/html and text/plain flavours, and test what actually survives a paste into Outlook desktop, Outlook web and the Salesforce email composer — these three differ, and Outlook desktop is historically the strictest about the HTML it accepts. Test in the browsers the reps use. Decide how much formatting is worth carrying: paragraph breaks and links are the realistic target, anything more is likely to be stripped.

Deliverable: A short compatibility finding and a recommended clipboard payload.

Unblocks: The outreach workflow, which is the whole value of the prepare-only decision.

9. Track E — News-Websites

One new top-level section that absorbs Settings → Sources and the source-precision panel, per §3.2.

E1 The curated German portal list L

Question: Which German news sources belong in the catalogue, and what is the verifiably correct feed URL for each?

Method: Build the list from a stated methodology rather than intuition — reach, relevance to ProAir's actual trigger classes, regional coverage, and trade press. Knowing the business sharpens this considerably: the national business dailies matter less than logistics and aviation trade press (DVZ, Verkehrsrundschau, Logistik Heute, and the English-language air-cargo titles), regional and local German press, which is where a plant fire or a site incident is reported first and often only, and union and industrial-action announcements from Verdi and EVG, which may be better served by their own pages than by any news feed. For each candidate, resolve and verify the actual feed: this is the handelsblatt.de versus rss.handelsblatt.com problem, and it is solved per source by fetching the feed, parsing it, and checking that it returns recent, well-formed items — not by guessing a URL pattern. Record for each source: canonical site, verified feed URL, update frequency, whether items are teaser-only, and whether it is paywalled.

Deliverable: A seed catalogue with every feed URL empirically verified, plus the written methodology so it can be extended later without me.

Unblocks: The whole section — this is its content.

Sizing note: this is L because verification is per-source manual work, not because the list is hard to imagine. It is also the item most amenable to being cut down — a well-verified list of 25 sources beats an unverified list of 100.

E2 Feed auto-discovery, including company newsrooms M

Question: Can we reliably find a company's own press feed from its name alone — the Mercedes newsroom case?

Method: Test a layered approach and measure each layer's hit rate: standard RSS autodiscovery via <link rel="alternate">; common newsroom path conventions (/presse, /newsroom, /media, /press-releases); and only then a Mistral call to propose a likely URL, since a model proposing URLs will confidently invent ones that do not exist. Every proposed feed must be fetched and validated before it is offered — the model suggests, the fetch decides.

Security constraint: this makes the backend fetch URLs chosen by a third party, which is exactly what services/safe_fetch.py and the admin-gated, default-off google_news_resolve_urls_enabled / google_news_fetch_snippets_enabled flags exist to contain. Any discovery feature must go through that same SSRF guard, and the research must confirm it covers this new call path.

Deliverable: Measured hit rate per layer, a validation procedure, and a cost figure for the Mistral layer against the €20 budget.

Unblocks: Whether auto-discovery is offered at company creation or stays a manual "find feed" button.

E3 Legal — storing and showing teasers S

Question: What may we store and display from German press feeds?

Method: Research the press publishers' ancillary right (§87f ff. UrhG, DSM Article 15) as it applies to storing headlines and teaser text and showing them in an internal tool and an internal email. Note the internal-use context materially reduces exposure compared to a public aggregator, but the digest is still a reproduction. Check terms of use for the specific sources shortlisted in E1, since some feeds carry explicit restrictions.

Deliverable: A short written position on what is safe to store, display and email, and any source that should be excluded on its terms.

Unblocks: E1's final list.

E4 Absorbing three source surfaces into one M

Question: How do the existing source controls fold into the new section without losing function?

What has to merge: Settings → Sources (frontend/src/pages/settings/SourcesTab.tsx: provider toggles, rate limits, workspace allow/denylist), the per-entity allowlists on companies and topics (with their non-obvious inheritance rules — allowlist replaces, denylist unions), and the source-precision panel (SourcePrecisionPanel.tsx, backed by services/source_precision.py, which already computes per-publisher precision and suggests denylist candidates). Three concepts, three current homes, one target section.

Method: Map every existing control to its new home. Design the browse → preview → attach flow, where attach targets the workspace default, a company, or a topic. Research how to make the allowlist-replaces / denylist-unions inheritance legible in a UI — it is currently a comment in the model and effectively invisible to users. Confirm nothing in the current admin-only surface becomes user-editable by accident, since the section is user-facing.

Deliverable: An information architecture with a control-by-control migration map.

Unblocks: Implementation, and prevents the "now there are four places to manage sources" outcome.

E5 Feed health over time S

Question: How do we notice when a feed silently dies?

Method: Feed URLs rot — sites redesign and the old feed 404s or, worse, keeps returning stale items. Define health checks (last successful fetch, last new item, parse errors) and where they surface. Check what the existing news_source_usage_logs and ingestion-run records already capture.

Deliverable: A health model and a surfacing decision.

Unblocks: Keeping E1's catalogue trustworthy after launch.

10. Track F — Archive and retention

F1 Suppression without paying twice M

Question: How do we implement "never show me this again" so the article does not come straight back?

The trap this item exists for: ingestion dedupe works by checking whether an Article row with that URL already exists (backend/app/services/ingestion.py:520). Delete a row and the next run re-fetches, re-embeds, re-triages and re-summarizes the same article — paying Mistral again and resurfacing old news as a new signal. Archiving rather than deleting, as decided, avoids this by construction, since the row survives to be matched. The research is in making sure the dedupe path treats archived rows as "seen" and not as absent.

Method: Trace every code path that queries Article and classify it as "should see archived rows" (dedupe, cost accounting, precision stats) or "should not" (feed, digest, timeline). Decide whether archive is a flag on Article, on Signal, or both — the existing SignalStatus.ARCHIVED already covers part of this and should not be duplicated. Define whether suppression is per-article or extends to near-duplicates of it, since a syndicated story arrives many times.

Deliverable: A state model for article and signal lifecycle, with the query-by-query audit.

Unblocks: Implementation, and prevents a subtle recurring AI bill.

F2 Archived signals with live todos S

Question: What does a rep see when a signal they have an open todo on gets archived by the other rep?

Method: As decided, the todo stays and gets a warning icon with an explanatory tooltip. Research the surrounding cases: what the todo list shows, whether the archived signal is still reachable from it, and whether favourites behave the same way. With two users sharing one catalogue, one rep archiving another's active work is a real scenario, not a theoretical one.

Deliverable: The interaction spec including the tooltip copy in both languages.

Unblocks: Implementation.

F3 Automatic retention S

Question: What does "delete everything older than X days" actually target?

Method: As decided, noise-only by default — articles that never produced a signal. Research the exact predicate (which skip_reason values, and what to do with articles whose signal was dismissed), where the retention window is configured, whether it also covers ThemeMatch rows, and whether the job is scheduled or manual. Confirm against F1 that whatever is purged cannot be re-ingested at cost — this is the one place where real deletion happens, so the tombstone question lands here.

Deliverable: A retention policy with the exact predicate and a cost-safety argument.

Unblocks: Implementation.

My recommendation: never purge an article that produced a signal a rep acted on. That destroys the audit trail behind a Salesforce Task, and disk is not the constraint here.

11. Track G — German throughout

G1 Inventory and contract M

Question: What exactly is still English, and what mechanism translates it given both languages stay supported?

Current state: the frontend is fully internationalised — eight namespaces, both locales, a language switcher, per-user preference. But frontend/src/i18n/locales/de/errors.json is literally {}, there is no error_code anywhere in the backend, and all 68 detail= strings are English free text. docs/german-i18n-planning.html §8 already designed the error-code contract for exactly this and it was never built.

Method: Inventory every user-visible English string: the 68 API details; FastAPI/Pydantic 422 validation bodies, which are structurally English and which users hit constantly; the digest email template; and any AI-produced text that reaches the UI, noting that triage_reason comes back from Mistral and follows the workspace output-language setting rather than the UI locale — that distinction needs deciding explicitly. Then re-validate the §8 error-code design against the current codebase: it was written before several of these endpoints existed.

Deliverable: A complete string inventory and a confirmed or revised error-code contract.

Unblocks: Implementation, which is then mechanical.

Note on the 422s: these are the ones users actually hit — every mistyped form field — and they are also the hardest, because the message is generated by Pydantic rather than written by us. Translating them means mapping validation error types to our own messages. If effort has to be cut here, cut the rare 502s, not the 422s.

12. Track H — Network events and indirect signals

The highest-upside track in this document, and the one that did not exist before we knew what ProAir sells.

The structural mismatch. Every retrieval and reasoning path in NewsAtlas assumes the article is about the company you want to sell to. TargetCompany holds keywords used to search for that company by name; the summarization prompt's company_mentioned check (ai_client.py) exists specifically to discard articles where the target is only a coincidental keyword match; and the theme path's extracted_company_name pulls out the company the article is centred on. For cargo charter that is systematically the wrong company. A fire at a tier-one supplier is a signal about every OEM downstream of it — and the pipeline is built to discard exactly that kind of article, because the OEM is not mentioned in it.

H1 Do the triggers even name the buyer? M needs measurement

Question: For ProAir's real trigger events, how often is the eventual buyer named in the article at all?

Method: Using the won-deal review from A4, take the events that caused real bookings and find the contemporaneous press coverage. For each, record whether the buyer was named, whether it was inferable from the article (a named supplier plus general knowledge of who it supplies), or absent entirely. Split by cargo and passenger, since the answer will differ sharply.

Deliverable: A percentage split across those three categories, per business line.

Unblocks: The entire track. If most triggers name the buyer, the current model is fine and H2 is unnecessary. If most do not, this is the most valuable thing v2 can build, and it should be sequenced ahead of most of Track A.

My expectation, stated so it can be proven wrong: passenger triggers usually name the buyer (an M&A deal, a tour, a tournament names the party travelling), and cargo triggers usually do not. If that holds, Track H is really a cargo-side track.

H2 From event to affected buyer L

Question: Can we get from "fire at supplier X" to "these three companies on our target list are exposed" reliably and cheaply enough to be worth it?

Method: Compare three approaches on cost and reliability against the €20 ceiling. (a) Ask the model: give it the event plus the target-company list and have it name who is exposed — cheapest to build, but supply-chain relationships are exactly the kind of fact a model will confidently invent, so measure hallucination rate explicitly rather than assuming. (b) Maintained relationship data: record on each target company its known suppliers, sites, ports and lanes, so exposure becomes a lookup rather than an inference — reliable, but only as good as data somebody has to maintain. (c) Geographic and sector proximity: an event at a location or in a sector flags targets with a known presence there — coarse, but cheap and hard to get catastrophically wrong. Test on the real events from H1, where the correct answer is already known.

Deliverable: A measured precision and recall figure per approach with a cost per event, and a recommendation. A hybrid is likely: cheap filtering first, model reasoning only on what survives.

Unblocks: Whether indirect signals ship, and in what form.

Honest risk: approach (a) is by far the most attractive to build and the most likely to produce plausible, confident, wrong answers. If a rep calls a customer citing a supply-chain exposure that does not exist, that costs more credibility than the signal was worth. Whatever ships must show the rep the evidence chain — this event, this named link, therefore this exposure — so a wrong inference is visibly wrong before the call, not after.

H3 Watching things that are not companies M

Question: How do we track ports, airports, airlines, rail operators, canals, regions and unions, none of which fit TargetCompany or ThemeWatch cleanly?

Method: Test how far ThemeWatch already stretches — it is query-term based and not company-bound, so "Hamburg port strike" may work today with no model change, which would be the cheapest possible answer. Establish where it breaks: its prompt still tries to extract a single company, and its match floor and per-run caps are tuned for topics rather than for rare, high-value events. Then decide whether a distinct watch type is warranted or whether the theme path plus a better prompt suffices.

Deliverable: A finding on whether the existing theme model covers event-watching, and a spec for whatever gap remains.

Unblocks: H2's input, and the alerting question below.

H4 Alerting for time-critical signals M

Question: Which signals justify interrupting a rep, and how fast can the pipeline actually deliver one?

Method: Measure the real end-to-end latency today from publication to a signal appearing — publisher lag, ingestion schedule, and processing. Determine which event classes from A4 have a decision window shorter than the digest cycle. Design the escalation rule conservatively: an alert that fires on anything less than a genuine opportunity trains two people to ignore it within a week, and with only two users there is no room for that. Decide the channel (email, push, something else) and the rate limit.

Deliverable: A measured latency figure, the escalation rule, and a channel recommendation.

Unblocks: Whether the disruption class gets a path faster than the daily digest, per the note in Track B.

Sequencing note: this is worthless until scoring discriminates. An alert built on a score that clusters at 3–4 fires constantly and gets muted. A4 and A2 first, then this.
