1. How to read this

Research items keep four parts, because a research task that does not name the decision it unblocks is just reading: Question, Method, Deliverable, Unblocks. Sizes are research sizes: S under a day, M one to three days, L more than three days.

Sections 3 to 7 are not research — they are decisions and proposals, actionable as written. §5 and §6 are new and want your review; §7 is a challenge to an idea you raised.

2.1 What the win/loss analysis changes

The ProAir win/loss review covers 1,605 opportunities across 588 accounts, January 2024 to August 2026. It was not built to answer this plan's questions and explicitly says so — "Zu Anlässen und Presseresonanz enthält der Datensatz keinerlei Information." But it settles a great deal that this plan had been guessing at, and it contradicts three things in it.

The uncomfortable finding: lead generation may not be the constraint. 1,605 opportunities over 31 months is roughly 52 enquiries a month for two reps. Of 880 losses, only 26 are genuine competitive defeats at a comparable offer. The rest are customer cancellations (213), no response at all (181), price (124), a different solution (113), and pure price indications (87). ProAir does not have a shortage of enquiries — it has a qualification and follow-up problem, which is exactly what the report's own four measures address. NewsAtlas produces more enquiries. If the reps are already at capacity, more leads may not convert into revenue.
Decided: acquisition. The question above was put to the business and answered — NewsAtlas is for generating more enquiries and finding new companies, not for adding context to enquiries already arriving. The concentration finding supports it: 54.8% of won volume sitting in ten accounts is a real strategic risk, and new-customer acquisition is the only thing in this plan that addresses it. Two consequences follow, both recorded in §2: Track H becomes the centre of gravity, and success is measured in new accounts opened rather than win rate — because a working acquisition product lowers the blended win rate by adding first enquiries, which convert at 23.3% against 41.0% by the third.
The capacity caution still stands, and should be watched rather than designed around. Two reps already handle ~52 enquiries a month and lose 181 deals to no response at all. Adding enquiries without addressing that will convert new leads into new no-responses. The four CRM measures in the win/loss report — indication and cancellation statuses, the offer-sent timestamp, mandatory passenger amounts, and follow-up automation — are outside this plan's scope but are the thing that lets acquisition actually land. Worth sequencing at least the follow-up automation alongside v2.

  • Urgency wins. Same-day ad hoc 57.5%, one to three days 48.7%, falling to 19–31% beyond a week. Cargo's median lead time is 41 days on won deals against 117 on lost ones. The disruption classes in §3.1 are pointed at the right kind of demand.

  • The third-enquiry threshold: 23.3% → 29.9% → 41.0%. Existing accounts convert far better than new ones, which cuts against prospect discovery and for the context-on-existing-accounts framing above.

One caution on the lead-time finding. It measures enquiry-to-flight, not event-to-enquiry, and the report itself flags it as correlation — short lead times may simply come from customers who had already decided. It supports focusing on urgency classes. It is not yet evidence that a 24-hour digest is too slow, so the no-alerting decision stands.

3. The charter signal taxonomy approved

The nine current types (ai_client.py:15) are a B2B software seller's list and name no charter trigger — very likely a direct contributor to the 3/5–4/5 clustering, since the model has no vocabulary for what makes one charter opportunity better than another. The replacement, with each type's default budget direction — what the event does to spend in our category, which is what replaces sentiment. Revised in this revision against §2.1: tour_or_fixture split, property_activity added, sports_fixture shipped disabled.

4. The Infrastruktur entity type approved

Named "Infrastruktur" (EN "Infrastructure"), in the top navigation beside Unternehmen and Themen. The conceptual line: a company is someone we sell to; a topic is a subject we follow; this is something we watch because its disruption moves freight or people — never itself a prospect. Breadth is carried by a required kind field rather than a longer section name.

6. The 0–10 relevance scale for review

Designed to your anchors: 10 reserved and rare, 7–9 the meaningful band, 0 meaning no alignment at all. The scale folds two questions into one number — how strong is the event and how well does it fit what ProAir sells — and the required rationale text is what keeps that legible.

ScoreMeaningTest the model must apply
10ReservedA named, dated, confirmed transport need ProAir can serve directly, with an identifiable buyer. Expect a handful per year, not per month. If it is being awarded often, the anchor has drifted.
9Call todayClear trigger, buyer named, timing known. No research step needed before picking up the phone.
8Clear opportunityClear trigger and named buyer, but scope or timing uncertain.
7Worth pursuingCredible trigger; buyer named or confidently inferable with evidence. One research step before calling.
6Weak leadRight event type, but the buyer is not named and the inference is thin.
5Wrong scale or placeRight event type, wrong magnitude or a geography ProAir does not serve.
4Relevant company, no angleReal event at a tracked company with no transport implication — a leadership change at an OEM.
3BackgroundContext about a relevant company. Useful on the timeline, not for outreach.
2Industry noiseMarginally related sector news.
1Not relevantNo connection, but nothing contradictory either.
0No alignmentNothing to do with the business at all.
Digest floor: 7. This restates the agreed "top 10, never below 4/5" onto the new scale. Your own band definition puts meaningful signals at 7–9, so 7 is the floor that preserves the original intent. A linear conversion of 4/5 would give 8, which would be stricter than what was agreed — flagging the difference rather than silently picking one.
One refinement I would make to your definition of 0. You defined 0 as no alignment and negative overall sentiment. I would keep 0 for "irrelevant" only, and let buyer_distress carry the negative case through its type and its freezes budget direction instead. Reason: a target's insolvency is highly informative — it should suppress outreach and appear on the timeline — and if it scores 0 it becomes indistinguishable from an article about a football match. Same information, better preserved.
Migration. Existing signals carry 1–5 values. Do not linearly rescale them — a 3 under the old unanchored prompt does not mean 6 under the new anchored one, and rescaling would produce confident nonsense. Recommendation: keep the old value in a legacy column, exclude pre-migration signals from the new floor logic, and let history age out. A3's gold set gets labelled on the new scale from the start.

7. Serving both business lines challenge

You raised the idea of custom search types prefilled through AI analysis of company data, and asked me to challenge it. The intent is right and the mechanism is where I would push back.

The problem is real. A keyword search for "Mercedes" finds news about Mercedes, not the events that would make Mercedes need a charter. Different companies have genuinely different trigger profiles: an automotive OEM's are line-down and supplier fire; a pharma company's are cold-chain and recall; a construction firm's are project milestones and oversized equipment. And with equal effort going to both business lines, one generic query per company cannot serve both.

Three problems with "AI analysis of company data" as stated.

First, what data? NewsAtlas holds a name, keywords, an industry string and some allowlists. That is thin. A model asked to analyse it would really be drawing on its own prior knowledge of the company — fine for Mercedes, unreliable for the mid-size Mittelstand supplier it has never heard of. Quality would be inversely proportional to how much you need the help, which is the wrong way round.

Second, it may be the wrong layer. "Cargo and passenger need different searches" is better solved by the taxonomy that already has to exist than by a new entity. Third, generated terms nobody reviews become unexplainable — when a company stops producing signals, you cannot tell whether nothing happened or its generated terms were quietly bad.

What I would build instead: a per-company trigger profile. Three fields on TargetCompany — which business lines apply (cargo, passenger, both), which §3 event types are relevant, and which Infrastruktur entries it depends on (the §4 link table, which H2 needs anyway). AI pre-fills the profile at company creation; the rep reviews and corrects it. This gets the tailoring you want without a parallel search mechanism, reuses three things already being built, and keeps every generated value visible and editable — the same "propose, never silently apply" discipline that TopicTemplate and suggest_topics() already follow. Cost is not an objection: this is one call per company at creation, not per article.
A consequence of "equal effort" that needs a decision. A single top-10 digest will be dominated by whichever business line produces more signal volume — and cargo, with its disruption classes and far larger universe of trigger events, will almost certainly produce more. If both lines matter equally, the digest probably needs to be balanced explicitly (say, top 5 per line) rather than ranked globally. B2 now carries this question; B1's measurement will show how lopsided it actually is.

R0.3 The free tier's real limits resolved

Answered, and the news is better than feared. The alarm in revision 4 rested on a widely-reported "2 requests per minute" figure for the free tier. This account's actual limits are different, and the tier is workable without change to the €20 posture.

Tokens are not the constraint. Estimating a daily run at 250 embeddings, 250 triage calls and ~70 summarizations gives roughly 470,000 tokens — about 14m a month. Tripling that for themes and infrastructure entries still lands near 40m against a 100m monthly allowance. The stated 100m per month is comfortably sufficient, with room for the richer prompts this plan adds.

Requests are the constraint, and the tier's shape tells you the fix. mistral-large allows 250,000 tokens per minute across 4.2 requests — roughly 59,000 tokens per request. The tier rewards few large calls, not many small ones. Four changes follow, all of which A5 should validate against the gold set:
  1. Batch summarization. Ten articles per call at ~2,500 tokens each is 25,000 tokens, well inside the envelope. Seventy summaries becomes seven requests — roughly 100 seconds instead of 17 minutes. The single largest win available.
  2. Move triage to ministral-8b — 188 requests a minute against 50, and 12× the token headroom. Triage is a binary classification; measure whether an 8B model holds.
  3. Batch embeddings. The embeddings API accepts arrays, so 250 embeddings is a handful of requests rather than 250.
  4. Test mistral-medium-2508 for summarization. If quality holds against the gold set, that alone is 5.4× the throughput.
One thing to verify before relying on any of this. The workspace config uses the aliases mistral-large-latest and mistral-small-latest, while the limits are published against dated IDs. The list shows mistral-medium-latest at 25,000 TPM against mistral-medium-2508's 356,250 — a 14× difference for what reads like the same model. Aliases may resolve to entirely different limit entries, so pin dated model IDs in settings rather than trusting an alias to inherit the better numbers.

Remaining work: confirm the alias behaviour, then measure a real run against these limits once R0.1 exists. Folded into A5 rather than kept as a separate blocking item.

20. Appendix — the trigger backtest

Redesigned for the approved dataset: company names and dates only, no rep annotation. That turns the review from an interview into a backtest — for each booking, search the preceding weeks and see whether a trigger event was in the news. It directly simulates the question that matters: would NewsAtlas have caught this?

Without a control group this study cannot fail, and is therefore worthless. Take fifty won bookings from companies the size of ProAir's automotive accounts, search the forty-five days before each, and news will turn up for nearly all of them — large German companies are in the news constantly. A 90% hit rate would look like proof and mean nothing. The measurable is not "did we find news" but the lift: does a trigger event appear meaningfully more often before an enquiry than in a matched window where no enquiry followed?

20.1 Design

ElementSpecification
Cases~150 (company, enquiry-date) pairs. Include won and lost — both are demand events, which doubles the sample at no cost. Only "what converts" needs won-specifically, and this study is about what creates demand.
Controls~150 (company, random-date) pairs drawn from the same 588 accounts, restricted to dates with no enquiry within ±30 days. Same companies, so company size and media presence cancel out.
Window45 days before the date. Justified by §2.1's cargo lead times (median 41 days on won deals); a sensitivity check at 14 and 90 days shows whether the result depends on the choice.
BlindingMandatory. Shuffle the rows and strip the case/control label before anything classifies them. Cheap to arrange; without it the classifier — human or model — finds what it expects.
ClassificationFor each row: was there any news; did it match a §3 trigger type; which type; was the company the subject or a bystander. The last one is H1's question, answered as a by-product.
OutputsLift of cases over controls, overall and per §3 type. Per-segment lift if the industry column is supplied. A null result is a real and useful outcome.

20.2 Limitations, stated before the result

The backtest understates a live system, and I will not use that to explain away a null. Retrospective search of 2024–26 German regional press is poor, and regional press is exactly where a plant fire gets reported first and often only. A backtest sees what is still indexed; live ingestion sees the feed as it publishes. So a weak result is genuinely ambiguous between "news does not predict demand" and "the archive cannot show us the news that would have". Recording this in advance so it cannot be produced afterwards as an excuse.

Correlation is also not causation here in a specific way: an event appearing before an enquiry does not mean the customer acted on it, and ProAir's inbound-heavy profile means many enquiries would have arrived regardless. The study measures whether news would have been a usable leading indicator, which is the right question for a monitoring product, not whether news caused the booking.

20.3 The stronger version, running in parallel

Switch on ingestion for these accounts now and compare signals against enquiries arriving over the next eight to twelve weeks. Slower, but free of every archive problem above and methodologically far stronger — a live signal that precedes a real enquiry is direct evidence. It cannot gate the v2 build, but it should run alongside it, and by the time Track H ships it will have produced a real answer about whether discovery works.

Effort. No rep time at all, which suits the approved dataset. Roughly half a day to build the case and control sets and the search harness, and a day of classification and analysis. The forward study costs only the decision to start ingesting now.
