1. How to read this

Research items keep the same four parts, because a research task that does not name the decision it unblocks is just reading: Question, Method, Deliverable, Unblocks. Sizes are research sizes, not implementation sizes: S under a day, M one to three days, L more than three days.

Sections 3 and 4 are different — they are proposals to approve or correct, not questions. Everything in them is actionable as written.

3. The charter signal taxonomy approve or correct

The nine current types (funding, leadership_change, expansion, hiring_surge, layoffs, product_launch, partnership, competitor_mention, other, in ai_client.py:15) are a B2B software seller's list. None of them names a charter trigger. That is very likely a direct contributor to the 3/5–4/5 clustering: the model has no vocabulary for what makes one charter opportunity better than another, so it falls back on generic business importance.

Below is the proposed replacement, derived from how charter demand actually arises. Each type carries a default budget direction — what the event does to spend in our category — which is what replaces sentiment.

3.1 Cargo — urgency

The core of the cargo business. Something has gone wrong and freight has to move by air.

TypeDirectionWhat it catches
production_disruptioncreatesLine-down, plant halt, output stoppage — at a manufacturer or anywhere in its inbound chain.
supplier_incidentcreatesFire, explosion, flood, structural failure at a production or logistics site. Reported fast, often only in regional press.
industrial_actioncreatesAnnounced or active strike affecting transport or production. The best-fit signal class available: Verdi and EVG announce days ahead, dated and precisely scoped, and it creates immediate, predictable air-freight demand.
transport_disruptioncreatesPort or airport congestion, rail closure, canal blockage, border and customs backlog, airspace closure, low-water on inland waterways.
insolvency_upstreamcreatesA supplier or logistics provider filing, forcing re-sourcing and expediting. Distinct from buyer_distress below, which is the opposite signal.
product_recallcreatesRecall requiring urgent parts or product movement.
disaster_reliefcreatesNatural disaster or humanitarian response.

3.2 Cargo — projects

Planned, high-value, long lead time. The class the daily digest serves best, and the one where an untracked company most often turns into a new customer.

TypeDirectionWhat it catches
major_projectcreatesLarge infrastructure, energy or industrial project awarded, launched or hitting a milestone. The contractor-discovery case: a project of this size publishes a site naming its contractors, and those contractors are the prospects — see H2.
plant_investmentcreatesNew factory, production line or major machinery installation — the machinery has to arrive.
capacity_rampcreatesProduction ramp-up, new model launch, or market entry into a distant region needing expedited logistics.

3.3 Passenger

TypeDirectionWhat it catches
executive_travelcreatesM&A, roadshow, IPO, board or delegation travel.
vip_appearancecreatesCelebrity, artist or executive appearance; film and TV production.
tour_or_fixturecreatesMusic tour, sports fixture, tournament qualification, team or squad travel.
group_eventcreatesCorporate event, incentive trip, conference or trade-fair delegation.
airline_disruptioncreatesCarrier insolvency, route cancellation or airline strike leaving a group without capacity. Bad news for the airline, direct demand for a charter operator.
evacuationcreatesCrisis evacuation or repatriation — where a German operator with its own AOC has a specific advantage.

3.4 Suppressor and fallback

TypeDirectionWhat it catches
buyer_distressfreezesThe target's own insolvency, site closure or market exit. The one case where the original "bad news is not a sales signal" instinct holds exactly.
othernoneFallback. The share of signals landing here is the health metric for this taxonomy — a high rate means classes are missing.
Settings behaviour, as requested. All types — the seventeen above plus the nine legacy ones — live in one admin-managed list in Settings, each with an on/off toggle. The charter types ship enabled; the legacy nine ship disabled. Legacy types are retained, not deleted: existing signal rows reference them, and dropping enum values would orphan history. A disabled type is removed from the prompt's allowed list and filtered out of the feed and digest, so disabling is a real quality lever, not just a display setting.
Two honest caveats. First, seventeen classes is a lot to ask a model to discriminate; classification reliability falls as a taxonomy grows, and A4 measures it per class so weak ones can be merged. Second, this list is derived from how charter demand generally arises, not from ProAir's own history — which is exactly what open question 1 exists to fix. Expect it to change once real bookings are reviewed.

4. The third entity type approve or correct

Ports, airports, rail networks, canals, borders, regions, unions and airlines do not fit TargetCompany (we do not sell to them) or ThemeWatch (they are named entities, not topics). The clean conceptual line is:

A company is someone we sell to. A topic is a subject we follow. This third thing is something we watch because its disruption moves freight or people — it is never itself a prospect.

4.1 Name

Proposed: "Infrastruktur" (EN: "Infrastructure"), sitting in the top navigation beside Unternehmen and Themen. Reasoning: it is a plain word both reps will read correctly at a glance, it needs no explanation, and it does not carry the negative framing of alternatives like "Störquellen". Breadth is carried by a required kind field on each entry rather than by a longer section name — so the label stays short while the content stays broad.

The honest tension: a union is not literally infrastructure. It sits here because it is an actor that can halt infrastructure, and the typed list makes that read naturally in context. If that bothers you, the best alternative is "Netzwerk & Infrastruktur" — more accurate, less crisp in a navigation bar.

4.2 Kinds

Every entry is typed. The kind drives the icon, the default query terms, and which template group it appears under.

4.3 Frontend

  • List view mirroring Themen: name, kind badge, region, active toggle, last-event date, and a count of linked companies. Sortable by last event, because "what moved recently" is the scanning question.
  • Creation from a template gallery, reusing the existing TopicTemplateGallery component and the TopicTemplate table pattern (models/topic_template.py) — that table exists as rows rather than a hardcoded list specifically so curation happens without a deploy, and the same reasoning applies here. Ship a seeded catalogue so the section is useful on day one instead of an empty page with an Add button.
  • Detail view in three parts: what is watched (query terms, attached feeds, region), recent events, and — the important one — linked companies, the target companies whose exposure to this entity we have recorded.
  • The reciprocal link on the company page: "Infrastructure this company depends on." This is where the exposure data gets maintained, and it is what H2 reads to turn an infrastructure event into a named prospect.
  • Signals from an infrastructure event render with the affected company plus the evidence chain, never the bare assertion.
Reuse note: ThemeWatch already carries almost the whole shape needed here — query terms, exclude terms, per-entity source allowlist with inherit semantics, per-entity Google News edition, active flag, manual run cooldown. H3 asks whether Infrastruktur should be a new model or a typed variant of that one. A typed variant is far cheaper; the risk is overloading a model whose prompt still tries to extract a single company name.

5. Phase 0 — Measurement foundation Blocks everything

Confirmed gap: NewsAtlas has no data export. CSV is import-only (api/target_companies.py:162). The only read surface for raw pipeline data is GET /articles/skipped, admin-only and capped at 200 rows. Nothing data-driven is decidable today.

R0.1 Analytical export M

Question: What is the smallest export that answers every measurement question here without becoming a reporting feature we then maintain?

Method: Enumerate the needed fields — for articles fetched_at, published_at, source, source_name, skip_reason, triage_reason, content_enriched; for signals relevance_score, signal_type, confidence, status, token counts, emailed_at. One admin export per entity with a date range. Check what ai_usage_logs and news_source_usage_logs already cover.

Deliverable: Export endpoint spec.

Unblocks: Every measurement item below.

Recommendation: build it as a permanent admin feature, not a throwaway script. We re-measure after every prompt change in Track A, and a script needing database access each time will not get run.

R0.2 Baseline funnel and score distribution M needs measurement

Question: What does the pipeline actually do today?

Method: Run the export over full history. Produce: the relevance_score histogram; splits by signal_type, confidence, source and is_headline_only; articles fetched per company per month; drop rates at each funnel stage (URL dedupe → title collapse → cap → embedding dedupe → triage → summarize); dismissal rate per score band.

Deliverable: The baseline every Track A change is measured against.

Unblocks: A1, A2, A3, B1, C1.

R0.3 Cost model against the €20 ceiling S

Question: What does a run cost by stage, and what headroom does €20/month leave?

Method: Confirm current Mistral list prices from the vendor rather than assuming. Cross with the real per-signal token counts already stored. Model the marginal cost of this revision's additions specifically — the taxonomy expands the prompt's allowed list, score rationale adds output tokens, and evidence chains add more.

Deliverable: Cost per run and per month with a sensitivity table.

Unblocks: A2, A4, A5, H2.

Budget tension worth naming now: three things this revision added — the score rationale, the larger taxonomy, and the H2 evidence chain — all increase output tokens, which is the expensive direction. They are all justified, but together they may not fit €20/month alongside current volume. R0.3 quantifies it and A5 finds the savings; if it still does not fit, open question 9 is the decision point.

6. Track A — Scoring and taxonomy

A1 How many 3s are not judgements? S needs measurement

Confirmed defect: SummaryResult.relevance_score: int = 3 (ai_client.py:218). If Mistral omits the key or returns something unparseable, the signal silently becomes a 3. Same for confidence = "medium" and signal_type = "other". The signal_type and confidence validators at least log on an unrecognized value; a missing field logs nothing. We cannot distinguish "judged a 3" from "said nothing".

Method: Instrument field presence in the raw response, run one ingestion cycle, measure. Replay a sample to see how often the model returns malformed JSON.

Deliverable: The contamination rate, plus the fix — make the field required and treat absence as a retry or an error, never a silent middle score.

Unblocks: A2. If contamination is material, part of the clustering is a bug rather than a prompt problem.

A2 A rubric that discriminates, and says why M

Two design defects (ai_client.py:52, and the theme prompt at :147): only the endpoints are anchored — 5 is "a clear, immediate buying trigger… today", 1 is "tangentially related background news", and 2, 3 and 4 are undefined. A model given a 1–5 scale with unanchored middle values regresses to the middle-high band. And one number carries two questions: how strong is the event and how well does it fit our service.

Method: Write explicit behavioural anchors for all five levels, in charter terms, using the §3 taxonomy as the vocabulary. Add a required score rationale — one or two sentences stating why this score and not one higher or lower — stored on the signal and shown to the rep. Test whether splitting event strength from service fit improves spread enough to justify the extra output tokens. Measure spread and cost delta against A3.

Deliverable: A recommended rubric with measured spread, plus a recommendation on 1–5 versus 1–10 (see open question 8).

Unblocks: B1, and the digest floor.

Related: theme_match_min_relevance_score defaults to 3, placing the topic floor at the mode of the distribution, so it filters almost nothing. Company signals have no floor at all. Both need resetting once the rubric changes.

A3 A gold set, or we are guessing M

Question: What do the two reps consider a genuinely actionable charter signal?

Method: Export 100–150 real signals across the score range. Both reps independently label each act today / worth knowing / noise, with a reason and the business line it belongs to. Measure inter-rater agreement — disagreement is itself a finding. Build an offline harness that replays a prompt against the set and reports agreement, caching responses so an unchanged prompt costs nothing to re-run.

Deliverable: A labelled set committed to the repo plus the replay harness.

Unblocks: A2, A4, A5, H2, and all future prompt work. The most reusable artifact in the plan.

A4 Validating and tuning the taxonomy M

Question: Does §3 survive contact with reality, and can the model classify it reliably?

Method: Two passes. Backward: review ProAir's won bookings, ask what event caused each and whether it was in the press, and check that every one maps to a §3 type — anything that does not is a missing class. Forward: classify the A3 gold set and measure per-class reliability plus the share landing in other. Merge classes the model cannot separate. Then test whether budget direction is reliable enough to act on or should stay advisory.

Deliverable: A validated taxonomy with per-class reliability figures and the settings defaults.

Unblocks: The settings UI, Track C's good-time read, and Track H.

A5 Cost-quality parity per stage M

Question: Where is the best trade between tokens spent and quality gained?

Method: Three models already run (mistral_model, mistral_triage_model, mistral_embed_model). Using A3, sweep each stage: does a larger triage model reduce false negatives enough to pay for itself? Does a smaller summarization model lose measurable quality? What does the ai_feedback_note steering (services/feedback.py) contribute per token? Evaluate prompt caching and shorter system prompts as pure cost wins — which matters more now that §3 and the rationale both lengthen the prompt.

Deliverable: Per-stage recommendation with measured quality-per-euro, and a configuration that fits €20/month.

Unblocks: The budget envelope for everything else.

7. Track B — The daily digest

Rule: top 10, hard floor 4/5, always send. Today's digest instead mails every un-emailed signal ordered by score, filtered per user by follows (services/digest.py), with no floor at all.

B1 What the floor actually yields S needs measurement

Question: How many signals per day clear 4/5 today, and how does that change under the A2 rubric?

Method: Replay the rule against 90 days of history, before and after A2.

Deliverable: A distribution of daily digest sizes, including how many days send the empty-state line.

Unblocks: Confirmation that 4 is the right number, and the 1–5 versus 1–10 decision.

The floor is settled as 4/5, so this is no longer a go/no-go — it is calibration. If the answer is "three days a week are empty", that is information about the rubric and the source coverage, not a reason to lower the bar.

B2 Digest design M

Question: What does a rep need in the email to act without opening the app?

Method: Decide what each entry shows — signal type, score with its rationale, the evidence chain for indirect signals, and the copy-ready outreach text. Decide carry-over (a 5/5 nobody acted on yesterday: repeat or drop), whether this replaces the existing digest or runs beside it, and how per-user follow filtering interacts with a top-10 cap (top 10 globally, or per recipient). Write the empty-state copy in both languages.

Deliverable: A digest spec.

Unblocks: Implementation.

B3 Transactional email provider S

Current state: services/email_client.py is a thin smtplib wrapper. No bounce handling, no delivery tracking, no retry, no DKIM/SPF story in the repo. A failed send raises and that recipient's digest is lost silently. Now that the digest always sends, including on empty days, its reliability matters more, not less.

Method: Compare Postmark, SES and Brevo at this volume (2 recipients, daily) on free-tier limits, EU data residency, bounce and delivery webhooks, and effort against the existing EmailClient seam, which was built so the provider is a credential swap.

Deliverable: A recommendation with a cost line — likely inside a free tier at two recipients.

Unblocks: Whether B2 carries a provider migration.

8. Track C — Company timeline

Decided: a plain reverse-chronological list, with whatever data exists pointing to a good-time / bad-time read. No charts.

C1 Is there enough to read? S needs measurement

Question: How many signals does a typical tracked company accumulate per quarter?

Method: From R0.2, the per-company signal count distribution. max_articles_per_company_per_run defaults to 10 and triage discards more, so the ceiling may be low.

Deliverable: The distribution, and an honest finding on what share of companies have enough events for the read to mean anything.

Unblocks: Whether the good-time read is shown always, or only above a minimum event count.

Recommendation: below a threshold, show the list and suppress the verdict rather than printing a confident judgement drawn from two articles. A wrong "good time to approach" is worse than no verdict.

C2 How the verdict is derived S

Question: What rule turns a list of typed events into "good time" or "bad time"?

Method: Derive it from §3 budget direction rather than a new model call — recent creates versus freezes, weighted by recency and score. Keep it rule-based and explainable so the rep can see which events drove it, matching the transparency requirement on the score itself. Decide the window and whether cargo and passenger events are weighed separately.

Deliverable: The rule, with worked examples from real company histories.

Unblocks: Implementation. Costs nothing at inference time, which matters at €20/month.

9. Track D — Salesforce end-to-end

D1 Auth, objects and limits S

Method: Document the working connected-app flow as the reference. Confirm Sales Cloud API limits at this volume, the API user's required object permissions, and sandbox availability. Reuse the existing encrypted-settings pattern for credentials rather than inventing one.

Deliverable: An integration brief.

Unblocks: D2, D3.

D2 Account linking S

Resolved by the CSV. The export can carry the Account ID, so linking happens once at import, under human supervision, on data from Salesforce itself — never by fuzzy name at push time. That removes the failure mode that would have defined this track: a wrong match writing onto the wrong customer, or a missed match creating duplicate Accounts in the CRM of record.

Method: Confirm the export column and add it to the import column-mapping UI, which already guesses header names (services/target_company_import.py). Define behaviour for: a company added manually with no counterpart, an Account deleted or merged after import, and a re-import that changes the ID. Verify a stored ID still resolves before writing.

Deliverable: Import mapping spec plus unmatched/stale/merged behaviour.

Unblocks: D3.

Recommendation: a company without a Salesforce ID is not pushable. An explicit "not linked — re-import to link" state beats falling back to name matching, which would quietly reintroduce every risk the ID removes.

D3 Field mapping and idempotency M

Method: Define the mapping for both paths (create Account; create Task on an existing Account), including required Salesforce fields with no NewsAtlas equivalent. Design idempotency — an external key on the Task, or a local pushed-record log — so a double click or retry cannot create two Tasks. Define record ownership. Define the failure model: partial failures, validation-rule rejections, and how they surface. Decide what the Task body carries: the summary, the score with its rationale, and the evidence chain for indirect signals.

Deliverable: A field mapping table and a written failure and retry model.

Unblocks: Implementation.

D4 Copy-paste that keeps its formatting S

Method: Research the async Clipboard API writing both text/html and text/plain, and test what survives a paste into Outlook desktop, Outlook web and the Salesforce composer — these three differ and Outlook desktop is the strictest. Test in the browsers the reps use. Paragraph breaks and links are the realistic target; more will likely be stripped.

Deliverable: A compatibility finding and a recommended clipboard payload.

Unblocks: The outreach workflow, which is the entire value of the prepare-only decision.

10. Track E — News-Websites

E1 The curated source list L

Question: Which sources belong in the catalogue, and what is the verifiably correct feed URL for each?

Method: Build from a stated methodology, retargeted at ProAir's actual trigger classes rather than at general business news. Four groups matter: logistics and aviation trade press (DVZ, Verkehrsrundschau, Logistik Heute, the English-language air-cargo titles); regional and local German press, where a plant fire or site incident is reported first and often only; union and industrial-action announcements from Verdi and EVG, which may be better served by their own pages than by any news feed; and infrastructure operators' own channels (port authorities, DB, waterway agencies), which feed §4 directly. For each, resolve and verify the actual feed by fetching, parsing and checking it returns recent well-formed items — this is the handelsblatt.de versus rss.handelsblatt.com problem, and it is solved per source, not by guessing URL patterns. Record canonical site, verified feed URL, update frequency, teaser-only, paywalled.

Deliverable: A seed catalogue with every feed empirically verified, plus the written methodology so it can be extended without me.

Unblocks: The section's content, and §4's seeded entries.

Sizing: L because verification is per-source manual work. It is also the most cuttable item here — 25 well-verified sources beat 100 unverified ones.

E2 Feed auto-discovery, including company newsrooms M

Method: Layered, measuring each layer's hit rate: standard autodiscovery via <link rel="alternate">; common newsroom path conventions (/presse, /newsroom, /media, /press-releases); and only then a Mistral call to propose a URL, since a model asked for URLs will confidently invent them. Every proposal is fetched and validated before being offered — the model suggests, the fetch decides.

Security constraint: this makes the backend fetch third-party-chosen URLs, which is what services/safe_fetch.py and the default-off, admin-gated google_news_resolve_urls_enabled / google_news_fetch_snippets_enabled flags exist to contain. Discovery must go through the same SSRF guard, and the research must confirm it covers this new call path.

Deliverable: Hit rate per layer, a validation procedure, and the Mistral layer's cost against the €20 budget.

Unblocks: Whether discovery runs at company creation or stays a manual button.

E3 Legal — storing and showing teasers S

Method: Research the press publishers' ancillary right (§87f ff. UrhG, DSM Article 15) for storing headlines and teasers and showing them in an internal tool and an internal email. Internal use materially reduces exposure versus a public aggregator, but the digest is still a reproduction. Check the shortlisted sources' own terms.

Deliverable: A written position on what is safe to store, display and email, and any source to exclude on its terms.

Unblocks: E1's final list.

E4 Absorbing three source surfaces into one M

What has to merge: Settings → Sources (SourcesTab.tsx: provider toggles, rate limits, workspace allow/denylist); the per-entity allowlists on companies and topics, with their non-obvious inheritance (allowlist replaces, denylist unions); and the precision panel (SourcePrecisionPanel.tsx over services/source_precision.py, which already computes per-publisher precision and suggests denylist candidates). Three concepts, three homes, one target section — now with a fourth attach target, §4's Infrastruktur entries.

Method: Map every control to its new home. Design browse → preview → attach, where attach targets the workspace default, a company, a topic, or an infrastructure entry. Make the replace/union inheritance legible — it is currently a comment in the model and invisible to users. Confirm nothing admin-only becomes user-editable by accident, since the section is user-facing.

Deliverable: An information architecture with a control-by-control migration map.

Unblocks: Implementation, and prevents "now there are four places to manage sources".

E5 Feed health S

Method: Feeds rot — sites redesign and the feed 404s or, worse, keeps returning stale items. Define checks (last successful fetch, last new item, parse errors) and where they surface. Check what news_source_usage_logs and ingestion runs already capture.

Deliverable: A health model and a surfacing decision.

Unblocks: Keeping the catalogue trustworthy after launch.

11. Track F — Archive and retention

F1 Suppression without paying twice M

The trap: ingestion dedupe checks whether an Article row with that URL exists (services/ingestion.py:520). Delete a row and the next run re-fetches, re-embeds, re-triages and re-summarizes it — paying Mistral again and resurfacing old news as new. Archiving rather than deleting avoids this by construction, since the row survives to be matched. The research is making sure dedupe treats archived rows as seen.

Method: Trace every path that queries Article and classify it as "should see archived rows" (dedupe, cost accounting, precision stats) or "should not" (feed, digest, timeline). Decide whether archive is a flag on Article, on Signal, or both — SignalStatus.ARCHIVED already covers part of this and must not be duplicated. Define whether suppression extends to near-duplicates, since syndicated stories arrive many times.

Deliverable: A lifecycle state model with the query-by-query audit.

Unblocks: Implementation, and prevents a recurring AI bill.

F2 Archived signals with live todos S

Method: As decided, the todo stays and gets a warning icon with an explanatory tooltip. Research the surrounding cases: what the todo list shows, whether the archived signal stays reachable from it, and whether favourites behave the same. With two users on one shared catalogue, one rep archiving the other's active work is a real scenario.

Deliverable: The interaction spec including tooltip copy in both languages.

Unblocks: Implementation.

F3 Automatic retention S

Method: Noise-only, and — as decided — an article that produced a signal a rep acted on is never purged, because that destroys the audit trail behind a Salesforce Task. Research the exact predicate (which skip_reason values; what to do with dismissed signals), where the window is configured, whether it covers ThemeMatch too, and scheduled versus manual. Confirm against F1 that anything purged cannot be re-ingested at cost — this is the one place real deletion happens, so the tombstone question lands here.

Deliverable: A retention policy with the exact predicate and a cost-safety argument.

Unblocks: Implementation.

12. Track G — German throughout

G1 Inventory, contract, and the Pydantic question M

Current state: the frontend is fully internationalised — eight namespaces, both locales, a switcher, per-user preference. But locales/de/errors.json is literally {}, there is no error_code anywhere in the backend, and all 68 detail= strings are English free text. docs/german-i18n-planning.html §8 designed the error-code contract for exactly this and it was never built.

Method: Inventory every user-visible English string: the 68 API details; FastAPI/Pydantic 422 validation bodies; the digest email template; and AI-produced text reaching the UI — noting that triage_reason comes from Mistral and follows the workspace output language rather than the UI locale, a distinction that needs deciding explicitly. Re-validate the §8 error-code design against the current codebase, which has grown endpoints since. Then answer the Pydantic question specifically: translating 422s means mapping validation error types to our own messages, so scope the work by counting the distinct error types actually reachable through the forms — a small number would make this cheap, a long tail would make it the expensive part.

Deliverable: A complete string inventory, a confirmed or revised error-code contract, and a costed yes/no on Pydantic message translation.

Unblocks: Implementation, which is then mechanical.

Expectation: the reachable 422 types are probably few — required, type, min/max length, and a handful of custom validators — which would make translation feasible and worth doing, since these are the errors users actually hit. The decision rule you set stands: do it unless the overhead proves disproportionate, and G1's count is what proves that either way.

13. Track H — Indirect signals and prospect discovery

The structural mismatch. Every retrieval and reasoning path assumes the article is about the company we sell to. TargetCompany holds keywords to search for that company by name; the summarization prompt's company_mentioned check exists specifically to discard articles where the target is only a coincidental match; the theme path's extracted_company_name pulls out the company the article is centred on. For cargo that is systematically the wrong company — a fire at a tier-one supplier is a signal about every OEM downstream, and the pipeline is built to discard exactly that article.

H1 Do the triggers name the buyer? M needs measurement

Question: For ProAir's real trigger events, how often is the eventual buyer named in the article at all?

Method: Using the won-deal review from A4, find the contemporaneous coverage for each booking-causing event. Record whether the buyer was named, inferable (a named supplier or project plus a further research step), or absent. Split cargo and passenger.

Deliverable: A percentage split across those three categories, per business line.

Unblocks: How much of H2 is worth building, and in which order.

H2 From event to named prospect L

Question: Can we get from an event to a verifiable list of prospects, cheaply and reliably enough to be worth it?

Method: Two distinct problems, and the second is the more valuable one.

(a) Exposure of companies we already track. Compare: asking the model directly which tracked companies are exposed; a maintained relationship map (each company's known suppliers, sites, ports and lanes recorded in §4's link table, making exposure a lookup); and coarse geographic or sector proximity. Test against the H1 events where the right answer is already known, and measure hallucination rate explicitly rather than assuming it.

(b) Discovering companies we do not track — the contractor case. A billion-euro project publishes a site naming its contractors, and those contractors are prospects. Research the chain: from a major_project signal, locate the project's own site, extract named contractors and their roles, and present them as candidate companies with the source page as evidence. Reuse the E2 fetch-and-validate discipline and the same SSRF guard. This also covers the more general case raised in feedback — a company appearing in generic news that we simply do not know yet, and could approach about our services in general.

Deliverable: Measured precision and recall per approach with cost per event, and a recommendation. A hybrid is likely: cheap filtering first, model reasoning only on what survives.

Unblocks: Whether indirect signals and prospect discovery ship, and in what form.

Evidence chain is mandatory, as agreed. Every indirect signal shows the rep the path — this event, this named link or source page, therefore this company — in enough detail to verify before calling. A rep citing an exposure that does not exist costs more credibility than the signal was worth, so a wrong inference must be visibly wrong on screen, not discovered on the phone.

H3 Building Infrastruktur on what exists M

Question: Should §4's entity be a new model, or a typed variant of ThemeWatch?

Method: ThemeWatch already carries query terms, exclude terms, per-entity source allowlist with inherit semantics, per-entity Google News edition, active flag and manual-run cooldown — nearly the whole shape. Establish where it breaks: its prompt still tries to extract a single company, and its match floor and per-run caps are tuned for broad topics rather than for rare, high-value events. Test whether a typed variant plus a different prompt suffices, and what the migration and UI cost of each option is.

Deliverable: A model recommendation with the schema, plus the seeded catalogue of entries from E1.

Unblocks: §4's implementation and H2(a)'s link table.

H4 Prospect discovery into the workflow M

Question: What happens to a company NewsAtlas discovers that nobody is tracking?

Method: Design the queue: discovered companies with the event and evidence that surfaced them, deduped against the existing target list and against previous rejections so the same name does not reappear weekly. Decide the actions — add to targets, dismiss permanently, or push to Salesforce as a new Account — and how a discovered company reaches the digest, given it has no CompanyFollow and therefore no recipient under current filtering. Check what extracted_company_name on the theme path already produces, since that is a working start.

Deliverable: The discovery queue spec and its digest treatment.

Unblocks: The "get to know new companies through generic news" case, which is a genuine growth path rather than a refinement of existing coverage.
