NewsAtlas — Platform Usability & Onboarding Review

Can a non-technical person set up NewsAtlas — companies and topics — in the fewest possible clicks, with everything explained along the way? A full-platform pass measured against Nielsen's heuristics, Don Norman's The Design of Everyday Things, and the Laws of UX.

Scope: the entire frontend (login/signup, Dashboard, Signals feed, Archive, My companies, Topics, all Settings tabs) plus the onboarding checklist and topic template gallery specifically
Persona: a first-time, non-technical admin standing up a brand-new workspace alone, with no engineer nearby
Method: the app was actually run (Postgres + FastAPI backend + Vite frontend) and driven end-to-end with a real signup and setup flow via a scripted browser session, with screenshots captured at each step; findings are cross-checked against the source
Prior review: docs/ui-ux-review.html (all 13 findings marked fixed) covered general interaction polish — this review re-checks the app fresh and focuses specifically on first-run setup friction and plain-language clarity

Executive summary

NewsAtlas' actual setup mechanics are close to as lean as they can be: adding a company is a 3-click round trip, and applying a topic template is 2 clicks — genuinely best-in-class for this kind of B2B setup flow, and better than most SaaS onboarding. The onboarding checklist on the Dashboard correctly tracks real progress and points to the right pages. Field-level copy (helper text, placeholders, tooltips) is consistently written in plain, jargon-free language in both English and German.

The problem isn't clicks. It's that a first-time admin can follow the checklist exactly as written, tick off every step, click the big blue "Fetch new signals" button — and get nothing, with no explanation anywhere in the checklist, the Dashboard, or the Signals page about why. The actual cause (no news source is turned on yet) is one click away on the Topics page specifically, but invisible everywhere else a first-time user is actually looking. That single gap (F1) is the difference between "this took me two minutes" and "I gave up, thinking it's broken" for exactly the non-technical audience this review is optimizing for — everything else found here is real but secondary polish by comparison.

Click-count: setting up a company vs. a topic

Measured live, from a fresh empty workspace, to the point where the item is saved and visible in its list. Typing into a field doesn't count as a "click."

Adding a company (fastest path)

  1. Click My companies in the top nav
  2. Click Add company
  3. Type the company name (the only required field)
  4. Click Add target company
3 clicks

Every other field in the modal (industry, other names, topic terms, never-match terms, trusted sources) is optional and can be skipped entirely. This is a good, lean flow.

Adding a topic via the template gallery (fastest path)

  1. Click Topics in the top nav — a workspace with zero topics lands directly in the template gallery, no extra click needed
  2. Click Use this template on any card
2 clicks

This is the single best-designed flow in the product: zero fields, zero typing, a fully-configured topic (search terms, exclusions, and in some cases suggested sources) saved in two clicks. It should be the template for how the company flow and every other "create" flow in the app eventually feels.

Adding a topic manually, without a template

  1. Click Topics in the top nav (lands in the gallery)
  2. Click Back to your topics to escape the gallery
  3. Click Add topic
  4. Type a topic name and at least one search term (both required)
  5. Click Add topic to submit
4 clicks

One click more than the company flow, and the modal it opens onto is considerably denser (see F3) — reasonable, since the product clearly wants to funnel people toward templates first, but worth knowing this is the path anyone with a specific, un-templated topic in mind will actually take.

The step nothing above counts: turning on a news source

None of the click counts above produce a single real result unless a news source is also enabled. On a fresh self-hosted install, Google News RSS ships off by default and NewsAPI.org needs an API key that most non-technical admins won't have to hand during initial setup. Turning on the free option:

  1. Open the profile menu and click Settings
  2. Click the News sources tab
  3. Check Google News RSS
  4. Click Save settings
4 clicks

This is not counted in — or reachable from — the onboarding checklist at all. See F1.

The Design of Everyday Things

Don Norman's core vocabulary — applied to the setup flow specifically, since that's where a non-technical user's mental model is being built for the first time.

ConceptRatingNotes
Affordances & signifiersGoodButtons look clickable, disabled states look disabled, the checklist's open circles vs. green checkmarks clearly signify "not done" vs. "done." The Invite code field is the exception — it affords typing but signifies nothing about what to type (F5).
Conceptual modelPoorThe product's implied model is "add something → it starts finding news." The real model is "add something → and separately make sure a news source is switched on → then it finds news." Nothing in the UI teaches this second, necessary half of the model before the user needs it (F1).
MappingGoodThe relationship between a company/topic's settings and what it will match is generally well mapped — hint text like "Leave empty to get everything about the company" directly describes cause and effect.
FeedbackPartialToasts, the ingestion progress bar, and the live-updating checklist are strong, immediate feedback. The gap is delayed/negative feedback: the one time feedback matters most (a run that found nothing, for a structural reason the user can fix), it's demoted to small print in a run-history card instead of surfaced where the action was taken.
ConstraintsGood"Add topic" is correctly disabled until a search term exists; destructive actions require a second confirming click. Norman's forcing-function idea is under-used in one place: nothing stops (or even flags) a workspace from being "fully set up" per the checklist while structurally unable to produce a result.
Discoverability (Gulf of Execution)GoodLow gulf of execution for the core create flows — a user rarely has to guess how to start an action.
Discoverability (Gulf of Evaluation)PoorHigh gulf of evaluation after running ingestion: the system did something, but whether it worked, and why not, takes real digging (scrolling to a run-history card, reading a backend-flavored message) to understand.

Laws of UX

LawRatingNotes
Jakob's LawPartialTemplates, tag-input chips, and a live checklist all match patterns users already know from other SaaS tools. What breaks the expectation set by "every other onboarding wizard": completing every step without a working result (most tools either block that state or explain it inline).
Hick's LawPoorThe manual "Add topic" modal presents 3 grouped sections and ~9 controls simultaneously (F3) — measurably more decision surface than the company modal or the template-gallery path sitting one click away from it.
Fitts's LawGoodPrimary actions ("Add company," "Add topic," "Use this template," "Fetch new signals") are large, high-contrast, and consistently placed top-right — easy, fast targets.
Miller's LawPartialThe 3-step onboarding checklist respects working-memory limits well. The Company profile page (F2) does not: 7 fields of very different weight and purpose appear together with no grouping beyond two small all-caps section labels.
Doherty ThresholdGoodAll observed interactions (modal open, save, toast, list refresh) responded well under the ~400ms threshold in local testing; the ingestion progress bar correctly acknowledges the one genuinely slow operation instead of leaving the UI silent.
Aesthetic-Usability EffectGoodClean, uncluttered visual design earns real trust on first impression — which raises the stakes when that trust is spent on a checklist that quietly doesn't deliver (F1).
Von Restorff EffectPartialThe red "Google News RSS is disabled" banner on the Topics page does stand out well by color. It just doesn't exist on the two other pages (Dashboard, Signals) where a user is equally likely to be looking when they hit this problem.
Zeigarnik EffectGoodThe open-circle "unfinished task" checklist items create a genuinely effective pull to complete setup — exactly the effect this principle predicts, well used here.
Postel's LawGoodTag-style inputs (press Enter after each term) are forgiving of how a user enters multi-value fields, and optional fields default sensibly ("inherits workspace default") rather than forcing an explicit choice.
Tesler's Law (conservation of complexity)PartialReal, inherent complexity exists (sources, regions, relevance scoring) and has to live somewhere. Today it's pushed onto the user's screen essentially unfiltered on first contact (F3, F6) rather than absorbed by better defaults + progressive disclosure.

F1. The setup checklist can be completed end-to-end and still produce zero results, silently Critical

Onboarding checklist (frontend/src/components/SetupChecklist.tsx), Topics page banner (frontend/src/pages/ThemesPage.tsx), ingestion run-history (frontend/src/components/IngestionStatusPanel.tsx), default value of google_news_rss_enabled

What was observed

Starting from a brand-new signup: the Get Started checklist's 3 steps ("Describe your company," "Add a company or a topic," "Fetch your first signals") can all be completed exactly as instructed — the checklist correctly ticks each one off live. Clicking "Fetch new signals" then runs, finishes, and reports 0 new articles, 0 signals, 0 topic matches. The reason is only visible in a small run-history card that isn't part of the checklist at all:

Two independent, silent traps exist depending on which path the checklist's "Add a company or a topic" step sends someone down:

  • Companies fetch via NewsAPI.org by default, which needs an API key. A fresh self-hosted install has no key configured until an admin gets one and enters it — and nothing on the My companies page, the checklist, or the Dashboard warns about this before the first fetch attempt.
  • Topics can only fetch via Google News RSS, which ships off by default even though it needs no API key at all and could safely be on from the start. The Topics page itself does show a red banner about this (good) — but the banner doesn't exist on the Dashboard or Signals feed, so a user who came from the checklist (which links to /settings/targets and /themes?gallery=1, not the Topics list view where the banner lives) can easily complete a topic via the template gallery without ever passing the warning.

Why this matters for a non-technical user specifically

A technical user seeing "0 new articles" would think to check logs or config. The persona this review is optimizing for follows instructions literally: they did every checklist step, in order, and the product told them each one succeeded. The natural conclusion isn't "I need to go find a news-source toggle in Settings" — it's "this doesn't work" or "I did something wrong," with no next action offered. This is precisely the gap between Norman's gulf of execution (low here — actions are easy to start) and gulf of evaluation (high — understanding the outcome is not).

Recommendation

Treat "the workspace can fetch from at least one working news source" as its own explicit, checkable condition — surfaced in the same checklist, not buried in a run-history log:

  • Consider shipping Google News RSS on by default for new workspaces, since it needs no key and is the only source topics can use — this alone would remove the topic-side trap entirely for most new installs.
  • Add the missing-source/missing-key condition as a visible checklist item or a banner that appears on the Dashboard and Signals feed (not just the Topics list), the same way it already does on the Topics page — with a direct link to the fix, in the same style as the existing "Go to news sources →" link.
  • Rewrite the two run-history messages in user-facing language with a next step, e.g. "Acme Robotics GmbH: we don't have a working news source to check yet. [Turn one on in Settings →]" instead of surfacing NEWSAPI_API_KEY or the internal theme: identifier prefix (see also F4).

F2. Company profile mixes plain identity fields with unexplained technical tuning parameters High

frontend/src/pages/settings/CompanyTab.tsx — a single unstructured form under "Company profile"

What was observed

The page a new admin is sent to first ("Describe your company," step 1 of the checklist) opens with exactly the two fields that step promises — Company name and Offering description, both well-explained. Immediately below them, on the same page with no visual separation beyond a small all-caps label, sit five more fields that are workspace-wide operational tuning, not company description: daily digest send time, a per-company article cap, a per-topic article cap, a maximum active-topic ceiling, and a 1–5 "minimum relevance score" threshold.

Why this matters

The defaults for every one of those five technical fields are already sensible (10, 10, 10, 3) — nothing here needs to be touched during first-time setup. But because the page is titled "Company profile" and appears as step 1 of the checklist, a careful, literal first-time user (exactly this review's persona) is likely to read every field before moving on, not knowing which ones are safe to ignore. This is Miller's Law territory: 7 simultaneous fields of very different purpose, with only two small section labels ("IDENTITY," "DIGEST & LIMITS") to organize them.

Recommendation

Split the page so the checklist's "Describe your company" step visually ends where the checklist's promise ends — either a real second tab ("Digest & limits") or a collapsed "Advanced" disclosure under the identity fields, closed by default, so a first-time visitor sees exactly Company name + Offering description + Save, and everything else is there for later.

F3. The manual "Add topic" form front-loads all advanced options at once High

frontend/src/pages/ThemesPage.tsx — the "Add topic" modal, sections sections.criteria/sections.sources/sections.region

What was observed

Opening "Add topic" shows Topic name and Industry, then three full sections rendered open and visible simultaneously: Search criteria (search terms, never-match terms), Sources (a news-source radio choice, a trusted-sources radio choice, and a blocked-sources tag field), and Region & language (country, language). That's roughly 9 distinct controls on screen at once for what the checklist frames as a simple "add a topic" action.

The contrast is sharpest because the product already has a better answer sitting one click away: the template gallery gets a fully-configured topic saved in 2 clicks with zero visible fields at all (see the click-count section above). The manual form is the fallback for anyone whose topic isn't covered by a template, and it's considerably harder than the path it's a fallback for.

Recommendation

Show Topic name + Search terms + "Add topic" by default, with Sources and Region & language collapsed behind a single "More options" disclosure (auto-expanded only when editing a topic that already has non-default values set, matching the "don't hide what's already in use" principle used correctly elsewhere in the app, e.g. workspace-default placeholders).

F4. User-facing error text leaks internal naming that contradicts the UI's own vocabulary Medium

Ingestion run-history messages, e.g. backend ingestion service's per-item skip/failure strings surfaced verbatim in IngestionStatusPanel.tsx / the Signals feed's "Last run" card

What was observed

Every screen in the product calls this concept "Topic" — the nav item, the page title, the checklist copy, the modal title. The one place it doesn't is the run-history text seen after a fetch: "[theme:Automotive] skipped: none of the news sources this topic may use are enabled..." — mixing the internal identifier theme: with the correct product term "topic" in the same sentence, and doing so in the one message a confused first-time user is most likely to actually read carefully.

Recommendation

Route ingestion run-history strings through the same translation/vocabulary layer as the rest of the UI instead of interpolating internal identifiers directly, so "Topic" (or "Thema" in German) is used consistently everywhere a user-facing message mentions one.

F5. Sign-up asks for an "Invite code" with no explanation of what it is Medium

frontend/src/pages/Login.tsx — the sign-up form's Invite code field; frontend/src/i18n/locales/{en,de}/auth.json has no accompanying hint string

What was observed

Every other field on the sign-up form is self-explanatory (Name, Email, Password — the latter even has a visible "At least 10 characters" hint). "Invite code" has a label and nothing else, at the very first screen this product ever shows a new user.

Why this matters

NewsAtlas is self-hosted per-workspace: an invite code is a shared secret the person who stood up the server sets and hands out "out of band," per the project's own README. A genuinely non-technical end user — someone invited to join a workspace, not the person who deployed it — has no way to infer any of that from the field alone. This is a signifier with no signified meaning: the input affords typing, but gives no clue what belongs in it or where to get it.

Recommendation

Add a one-line hint under the field using the same field-hint pattern already used throughout the company/topic forms — e.g. "Ask whoever set up NewsAtlas for your workspace for this code."

F6. AI configuration exposes raw ML/ops parameters with no "you can skip this" signal Medium

frontend/src/pages/settings/AITab.tsx

What was observed

The AI configuration tab sits in the same tab row as Company, News sources, and Users — visually equally important — but its fields are Summarization model / Triage model / Embedding model as free-text strings (e.g. mistral-large-latest), a "Duplicate similarity threshold" numeric field (0.9), and an API key field, none of which a non-technical admin should ever need to touch, and none of which explain the consequence of getting them wrong (what happens at 0.5 vs. 0.9? what if the model name is misspelled?).

Recommendation

Either badge this tab clearly as advanced/optional ("Defaults work well for most workspaces — only change these if you know what you're doing"), or replace the free-text model fields with a constrained dropdown of supported models so a mistyped value can't silently break summarization.

F7. Three similarly-named source controls in one modal are easy to conflate Low

"Add topic" modal, "Sources" section — ThemeSourceSelector, SourceAllowlistField, and the source-denylist TagInput rendered together

What was observed

In the same section: "News sources for this topic" (which providers to query — Google News, NewsAPI, NewsData.io), "Trusted sources for this topic" (an allowlist of specific domains), and "Blocked sources" (a denylist of specific domains). All three are phrased around the word "sources," positioned back-to-back, and each is a fairly abstract concept without a concrete example visible until you start typing.

Recommendation

A short worked example under each ("e.g. only fetch from reuters.com and your local trade press") would ground the abstraction faster than the current before-and-after label/radio structure alone. Grouping "Trusted" and "Blocked" under one shared "Domain filters" sub-heading, visually distinct from "News sources" (which is about providers, not domains), would also reduce the chance of a user picking the wrong one.

What's already working well

The topic template gallery is the best flow in the app. Two clicks, zero typing, a fully-configured topic — this is the pattern the rest of the "create" experience should be measured against, not the other way around.
The onboarding checklist tracks real state, live. Adding a company immediately and correctly checks off "Add a company or a topic" without a page reload — exactly the kind of visible, trustworthy feedback loop the Zeigarnik effect predicts will keep a new user moving through setup.
Field-level hint copy is consistently plain-language, in both shipped languages. Sampled hint strings ("Leave empty to get everything about the company — add terms only if you're getting too much unrelated news") read the same register in German as in English — no jargon drift between locales.
Sensible, visible defaults instead of forced choices. "Use the workspace default" is pre-selected everywhere a per-item override exists, and placeholders spell out exactly what that default currently is (e.g. "Workspace default (US)") rather than leaving it implicit.

Open questions for you

A few calls worth your input before any of the above gets built:

  • Is it acceptable to change the shipped default for google_news_rss_enabled to true for new workspaces, or is there a reason (rate limits, terms of use, quality concerns per docs/google-news-quality-planning.html) it was deliberately left off?
  • For F1's checklist fix: would you rather block "Fetch new signals" entirely until a working source exists (a hard constraint), or just make the warning impossible to miss (a strong signifier) while still letting an admin fetch anyway?
  • Is the AI configuration tab (F6) meant to be visible to every admin, or should it eventually be gated further (e.g. behind a workspace-owner-only permission) given how easy it is to break summarization from there?
